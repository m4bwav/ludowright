I reviewed Tiny Colony's card balance and found one critical problem and five major ones. I treated it as a concept-stage design: a solo, turn-based deckbuilder with 12-turn runs, built around the promise that "every turn is a hard trade-off between growing and preparing".

1. **F-001, critical: Overclock is about four times too cheap** (`cards.csv:9`). The other nine cards follow one price rule exactly: 1 energy buys 2 output, 2 shield or 1 draw. By that rule Overclock (6 output + 1 draw) should cost 4 energy, but it costs 1. Every purchase would go to Overclock. I'd suggest cost 3 with output 4 and draw 1.
2. **F-002, major: gold piles up after turn 6** (`economy.md:3`, DESIGN rule 4). Four Mines make 8 gold a turn, but you can only buy one card a turn at 4–8 gold. A player buying the cheapest card ends the run with 28 gold they can't spend. It needs a second way to spend gold, or a cap on how much you can hold.
3. **F-003, major: Scout and Library are traps** (`cards.csv:7-8`). You draw 5 cards but have only 3 energy, so cards are already in surplus. These two spend scarce energy to get more of the thing you have too much of.
4. **F-004, major: shield can't be priced** (DESIGN rule 3). Nothing says how much damage events do, what they damage, or how a run is lost. That leaves Wall, Bunker and Fortress, and half of the core trade-off, without numbers.
5. **F-005, major (low confidence, needs a playtest): the trade-off wears away** (DESIGN rule 2). Buildings stay in play for free and energy never grows. Once your shield covers the event, every later turn is just growth.
6. **F-006, major: one "output" column covers gold and food.** Mine and Farm have identical rows, and Workshop, Overclock and Factory don't say what they produce.
7. **Two minor ones:**
   - **F-007:** colonists only eat. With one Farm, food runs out on turn 5, and the design doesn't say what happens then.
   - **F-008:** each big card is an exact multiple of a small one (Factory = 3 Mines, Fortress = 3 Walls), so they add no real choice.
